New command: `python -m koottam compare A B`.
- A paired comparison from cached answers: the flips each way, an exact McNemar p and a
  bootstrap 95% interval.
- Injection accuracy per true answer, and accuracy by how many council members were right.
- New test `test_mcnemar_counts_only_flips` (25 tests).

What it showed:
- **Supported overall:** 123 gained vs 84 lost, p = 0.008, +6.3 points (95% interval +1.8
  to +11.0).
- **Injection** +27.6 points (52 vs 20, p = 0.0002). **Cyber** +1.4 (71 vs 64, p = 0.61),
  which is noise.
- **The base said "attack" for 113 of 116 injection texts:** 0/56 safe texts right, 57/60
  attacks right. The student got 52/56 safe and 37/60 attacks.
- **Cause:** the agreement filter kept all 78 safe injection questions in the first 2,500,
  but only 37 of 50 attacks, so the training mix was 68% safe.
- **Teacher consensus, cyber:** where all 4 members were right, 69.5% → 73.3%; where 2 or
  fewer were, 37.1% → 25.7%.
- Extra checks from the cached answers (not in `compare`):
  - **Agreement with each teacher** rose from 57–63% to 68–69%.
  - **On the 32 questions** where at most one member was right, the student picked the
    members' most common answer 21 times.
  - **Answer order:** the base opens with its answer ("The correct answer is …") in 581 of
    616 replies; the student does in 0 of 616 and always reasons first. Median reply 494 →
    407 characters.
- The early 20-question smoke test (base 70%, student 60%) pointed the wrong way: 20
  questions is too few to read anything into.
